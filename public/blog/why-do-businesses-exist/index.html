One lakh years apart. Same bubble.

Change the year or the desire. The two bubbles never come apart.

Our desires have not changed. Food, sex, health, security. The caveman wanted them. You want them.

Businesses exist to fulfil them. That's the second idea in The Book of Clarity by Paras Chopra, the founder of Wingify, and I'm reading it one idea at a time.

The desire stays. Only the way we meet it changes.

Play with it

The real Maslow's pyramid

You've seen Maslow's pyramid: food and sleep at the bottom, then safety, love, esteem, and becoming who you want to be at the top. He redraws it with time running left to right. The tiers stay where they are. What moves is how each one gets met: better, faster, cheaper.

Even YouTube live is an old desire

Think about live streaming. What's the oldest desire it satisfies? Status. He writes that people go live to entertain, or to look desirable, and the real aim is status and prestige.

New screen. Same caveman, wanting the tribe to look at him.

The other question

What will not change in ten years?

Jeff Bezos says people ask him all the time what will change in the next ten years. Almost nobody asks what will not.

Everyone asks What will change in the next 10 years? Asked all the time
Nobody asks What will not change?
  • Low prices
  • Fast delivery

For Amazon the answer was easy. Nobody will ever want higher prices or slower delivery. So build on what stays the same.

Happy is never enough

"I'm happy!"

Customers are always dissatisfied, even when they say they are happy. In his 2016 letter to shareholders, Bezos calls them "beautifully, wonderfully dissatisfied".

That's why startups beat old companies. People keep looking for a better way to get the same old thing.

What fuels capitalism isn't the shiny new thing. It's the old thing, made better.
