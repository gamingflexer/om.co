Never believe your customers

Everyone starts the gym on Monday. Then a large cheese burst arrives. Watch what they do.

Om Surve·Founder, Ayu·4 min read

Sunday night. The plan is perfect.

We humans are notorious for saying one thing and doing another. In our heads, we're angels. In our actions, we go for love, money, fame and sugar.

That's the third idea in The Book of Clarity by Paras Chopra, who co-founded Wingify, and I'm reading it one idea a day. This one is about customers.

The biggest mistake

Believing what they say

One of the biggest mistakes a founder can make is believing what the customer says. Either they say they like your idea, or they hate it. Both are just words.

Customers vote with their wallets. Words are cheap. Money is expensive.

The few who stay at your real price are your evidence. Everyone else is a nice comment.

What I've learned

It's not about doing the interviews

In my startup, this is the lesson. It's not about doing user interviews. It's about how you do them.

Everyone doesuser interviews
What mattersknowing how

Here's the difference. Tap to swap the question.

Asks for words"Would you use this?"A free yes. It tells you how polite they are.
Looks for behaviour"What did you do last time?"Something they actually did, spent or tried.

The best guides on this are in the captions below the reel.

The rule

Don't ask. Watch.

The person who says they want to lose weight is the one buying the junk food. So don't ask people what they want. Watch what they do, and what they pay for. What they say and what they do is very different.

Your turn

Do this today

Pick one person who told you they like your idea. Ask for something small and real: money, a pre-order, an hour of their time, an introduction. Then write down what they did, not what they said.
