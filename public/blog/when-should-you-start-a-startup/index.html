Most of us start the day the idea shows up. We buy the domain, tell two friends, and start building.

That day is the NOT YET page. You start when two other things are true: you can do something people want, and nobody else does it well.

That's chapter one of The Book of Clarity by Paras Chopra, the founder of Wingify. The chapter is called "Capitalism rewards rare and valuable". Here's what I took from it.

Tap the drawing

Three circles. Where's the value?

He draws three circles. What you're good at. What people desire. What competitors are good at.

Tap the spot where you think the value is.

Tap anywhere inside the circles.

When I first read this, I said the value is in the middle, where all three meet. Wrong. The middle is where everyone already is, and in his drawing it gets a sad face.

The value sits where what you're good at meets what people want, and competitors are missing. He calls it the diamond.

Your mix is the rare part

He borrows a piece of career advice from Scott Adams, the Dilbert cartoonist: get good at several skills until nobody else has your exact mix.

skill+skill+skill=your mix

Startups work the same way. Lots of people have one skill. Very few have your mix. That's where rare comes from.

Why it's hard

Two mistakes we all make

1

We build what we want.

He writes that a common, fatal mistake is misjudging what makes something valuable. Founders scratch their own itch and assume everyone has it.

Most people are not founders like us. What they want from life is very different.

2

We forget people are lazy.

Why would anyone leave something that already works for something they've never heard of?

what already works→you
"People—you, me, everyone else—are lazy." Paras Chopra

So "a bit better" isn't enough. It has to be worth getting off the sofa for.

The word we never learned

So it has to be both, rare and valuable. For that you need one word we never learned in our tier-3 colleges: insight. Our mentor taught us what it means.

The book is honest about how hard this is. Finding an idea that's both rare and valuable takes three things, he writes:

1InsightSpotting what people want that others haven't figured out yet.
2IterationsTrying, getting it wrong, and trying again.
3LuckThe part you don't control.

And that, he adds, is why the startups that make it are rare and valuable too.
